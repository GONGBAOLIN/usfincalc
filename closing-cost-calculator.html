Closing cost breakdown

How your closing costs split across lender fees, title/settlement, government charges, and prepaid escrow items.

What are closing costs?

Closing costs are the fees and charges — separate from your down payment — that you pay to finalize a mortgage and transfer property ownership. They typically total 2–5% of the purchase price and are due at the closing table. On a $400,000 home, expect $8,000–$20,000 depending on your state and loan type.

The four categories

Lender fees. Origination charges (often ~0.75–1% of the loan), appraisal ($400–$700), credit report, and flood certification. If you buy discount points to lower your rate, those go here too.

Title & settlement. Owner's title insurance (protects your ownership claim), lender's title insurance (required), title search, and the settlement/escrow agent's fee. Title insurance alone can run 0.5–1% of the price.

Government fees. State and local transfer taxes plus deed recording fees. This is where states diverge most: Texas charges no state transfer tax, while Delaware charges 4% and Pennsylvania 2%.

Prepaids & escrow. Not fees, but money paid upfront: prepaid mortgage interest for the partial first month, plus initial deposits into your escrow account for property taxes and homeowners insurance (usually 2–3 months' worth).

FHA and VA differences

FHA loans require an upfront mortgage insurance premium (UFMIP) of 1.75% of the loan amount. VA loans charge a funding fee of 1.25–3.3% (2.3% for first-time use with less than 5% down), though some veterans are exempt. Both can be rolled into the loan balance rather than paid at closing.

How to reduce closing costs

Negotiate seller concessions (common in buyer's markets), shop lenders and compare Loan Estimates line-by-line, ask about lender credits (accept a slightly higher rate for reduced upfront costs), and compare title insurance providers — you're allowed to choose your own in most states.

Frequently asked questions

How much are closing costs on a house?

Typically 2–5% of the purchase price. On a $400,000 home, that's $8,000–$20,000. The largest variables are state transfer taxes, loan type (FHA/VA add upfront fees), and whether you buy discount points.

What's included in closing costs?

Lender fees (origination, appraisal, credit report), title fees (owner's and lender's title insurance, search, settlement), government charges (transfer tax, recording), and prepaid items (property tax escrow, insurance escrow, prepaid interest).

Who pays closing costs — buyer or seller?

Both, for different items. Buyers pay lender fees, title insurance, appraisal, and prepaids. Sellers typically pay the real estate commission (5–6%) and sometimes transfer taxes. Buyers can negotiate seller concessions to offset costs.

Can I roll closing costs into the loan?

On a refinance, usually yes. On a purchase, generally no for conventional loans — but you can negotiate seller-paid concessions or take a lender credit (higher rate in exchange for the lender covering some costs). FHA UFMIP and VA funding fees can be financed.

Why do closing costs vary so much by state?

Transfer taxes are the main driver. Texas and California have low or no state transfer tax; Delaware (4%), Pennsylvania (2%), and Washington (1.8%) charge significant rates. Attorney-closing states and title insurance requirements add further variation.
