How this calculator works

This tool is based on the USDA's Expenditures on Children by Families report, which tracked actual spending by thousands of American families across seven categories: housing, food, transportation, clothing, healthcare, childcare/education, and miscellaneous. We've adjusted these figures for 2026 inflation and allow you to customize by income level, region, childcare choice, and sibling discount.

The $310,000+ figure explained

The headline number — over $310,000 from birth to 17 for a middle-income family — is not a scare tactic. It's the sum of housing (the largest share at ~29%), food, transportation, childcare, healthcare, clothing, and miscellaneous expenses attributed to the child. It does not include college, which adds $100,000–$220,000 depending on school type.

Cost factors

Income. Higher-income families spend more in absolute terms — larger homes, more activities, private lessons, travel — though as a percentage of income the burden is lower. The USDA divides families into three tiers; we use a continuous scale.

Region. Northeast and West costs run 8–12% above the national average due to higher housing and childcare markets. Midwest and South run 5–8% below.

Childcare. This is the most variable category. A full-time nanny can cost $30,000–$50,000/year in major metros, while a stay-at-home parent reduces this line to near zero (though with an opportunity cost not modeled here).

Siblings. The USDA found that each additional child reduces per-child costs by about 24% through shared bedrooms, hand-me-downs, and bulk economies.

What this doesn't include

Opportunity cost of reduced work hours, fertility treatments, adoption fees, extracurricular activities beyond the miscellaneous category, and private K–12 tuition. The actual financial impact of a child is higher than this calculator shows for many families.

Frequently asked questions

How much does it cost to raise a child in the US?

A middle-income family (earning $80,000–$130,000) spends approximately $310,000–$330,000 from birth to age 17 in 2026 dollars. Add $100,000+ for a 4-year public university. Higher-income families in expensive regions can spend $500,000+.

What is the biggest expense in raising a child?

Housing is the single largest category at roughly 29% of total costs — the additional bedroom, utilities, and furnishings attributed to the child. Childcare dominates in the early years (ages 0–5), while food and transportation grow as children become teenagers.

Does it cost less to raise a second child?

Yes — about 24% less per child according to USDA data. Shared bedrooms, hand-me-down clothing, bulk food, and reused gear all reduce marginal costs. Three or more children see further per-child reductions.

How much does childcare cost per year?

Average daycare center: $10,000–$17,000/year. Nanny: $30,000–$50,000+/year in metros. Family/informal care: $3,000–$8,000/year. A stay-at-home parent eliminates this cost but forfeits income and career growth — an opportunity cost often exceeding the childcare savings.

How does inflation affect the total cost?

At 3% annual inflation, a $15,000 annual cost in year 1 becomes over $25,500 by year 17. Over 18 years, inflation adds roughly 30–35% to the nominal total compared to holding costs constant. This calculator inflates all categories forward to show real future-dollar impact.
