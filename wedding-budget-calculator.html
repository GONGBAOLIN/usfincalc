How this wedding budget calculator works

The calculator takes your total budget and distributes it across 10 standard vendor categories using percentages derived from US wedding industry data (The Knot, Zola, WeddingWire annual surveys). The key insight: not all categories scale the same way with guest count. Catering is almost entirely per-head, while photography is a flat fee regardless of whether you invite 50 or 250 people.

When your guest count differs from the 120-person US baseline, per-guest categories (catering, stationery, favors) scale proportionally. Fixed-cost categories stay constant. All percentages are then re-normalized to 100% so your entire budget is allocated. The effect: a 50-person wedding shifts budget away from food and toward photography and decor; a 200-person wedding concentrates spending on catering.

A worked example

Suppose your budget is $40,000 with 150 guests and a wedding in 18 months, with $5,000 already saved. The guest count is 25% above baseline, so catering scales up from 25% to about 27% of budget (~$10,800). Photography stays at its base percentage (~11%, or ~$4,400). Your per-guest cost is $267. You need to save the remaining $35,000 over 18 months — about $1,944 per month.

How to use these numbers

Treat the breakdown as a starting framework, not a prescription. If you care deeply about photography, pull budget from favors or flowers. If you're having a backyard wedding, the venue line drops to nearly zero and everything else gets more room. The percentages reflect what couples typically spend — not what you must spend.

The savings timeline is the most actionable number: set up automatic transfers of that monthly amount into a dedicated wedding fund. If you're also saving for a home, compare this with our home affordability calculator to make sure both goals fit your income. And for understanding how wedding expenses affect your take-home pay, try the paycheck calculator.

Frequently asked questions

How much does the average US wedding cost?
The average US wedding cost is approximately $33,000–$35,000 as of 2025 (source: The Knot Real Weddings Study). This varies significantly by region — weddings in metro areas like New York or San Francisco often exceed $50,000, while weddings in rural areas may be well under $20,000.
What percentage of the wedding budget should go to the venue?
Venue and rentals typically account for about 30% of a wedding budget. This is the single largest line item and often includes the ceremony site, reception space, tables, chairs, and linens. All-inclusive venues may bundle catering into this cost.
How far in advance should I start saving for a wedding?
Most financial planners recommend 12–18 months of dedicated saving. Starting earlier reduces the monthly amount needed. For example, saving $30,000 over 12 months requires $2,500 per month, but spreading it over 18 months drops that to about $1,667 per month.
What wedding costs are often forgotten?
Commonly overlooked costs include vendor tips (usually 15–20% of each vendor's fee), marriage license fees, alterations for wedding attire, day-of coordination staff, overtime charges for venues and DJs, guest hotel room blocks, and post-wedding brunch. A 3–5% buffer in your budget helps cover these surprises.
Is this wedding budget calculator financial advice?
No. This calculator provides estimated budget allocations based on US national averages. Your actual costs will vary by region, season, vendor choices, and personal priorities. Use it as a starting framework, then adjust categories to fit your wedding vision.
