Home buying

Buying a home is the largest financial decision most Americans make, and the advertised monthly payment rarely tells the whole story. Property taxes, homeowners insurance, and — if you put down less than 20% — private mortgage insurance can add hundreds of dollars a month. These guides explain the pieces buyers most often underestimate, and pair with our Mortgage and Home Affordability calculators.

Income & tax

Few topics generate more confusion than how US income tax actually works. Marginal brackets get mistaken for flat rates, state taxes swing take-home pay by thousands of dollars a year, equity compensation is withheld at a rate that often doesn't match what you really owe, and the calendar quietly decides how investment gains are taxed. These guides untangle the mechanics — each with worked examples you can check against the Paycheck, RSU Tax, and Capital Gains calculators.

Understanding Tax Brackets Why a raise into a "higher bracket" never cuts your take-home pay. Take-Home Pay by State How state income tax changes what actually lands in your account. RSU Taxes Explained The 22% withholding trap and why RSUs can cost you at filing. Long-Term vs. Short-Term Capital Gains How one year of patience changes the tax on an investment gain. The HSA Triple Tax Advantage The only account that's tax-free going in, growing, and coming out.

Auto finance

Dealers negotiate around a monthly payment; smart buyers think in total cost. Sales tax, loan term, interest rate, and trade-in value all shift what a car really costs — sometimes by more than the sticker discount you fought for. This guide covers the 20/4/10 budgeting rule and the expenses beyond the loan, with the math handled by our Auto Loan Calculator.

Debt payoff

Federal student loans follow a fixed amortization formula, which means your payment — and the total interest you'll hand over — is predictable to the dollar. That predictability is leverage: even $50 of extra principal a month can cut years off a standard 10-year plan. These guides estimate your payment before servicers do, compare payoff strategies once you're in repayment, and explain the interest mechanics that make balances grow. All connect to the Student Loan Calculator.

Student Loan Payoff Strategies Avalanche vs. snowball, and when extra payments beat investing. How Much Will My Student Loan Payment Be? The repayment formula, 2026 federal rates, and what moves your bill. Interest Accrual & Capitalization Why a balance can grow while you pay — and how to stop it.

College saving

A 529 plan is the closest thing to a retirement account for education: contributions grow tax-deferred and withdrawals for qualified expenses are tax-free. The catch is that tuition inflation runs ahead of general inflation, so the target keeps moving — and the tax-free promise only holds when withdrawals pay for the right things. These guides cover both the growth math and the spending rules behind our 529 College Savings Calculator.

529 Plan Growth Projections How a monthly contribution, time, and fees turn into a college fund. What Counts as a Qualified 529 Expense? What the fund can pay tax-free, what it can't, and the real penalty math.
