How HSA tax savings work

A Health Savings Account is the most tax-favored account in the US code, and this calculator quantifies why. When you contribute, three things happen. Your contribution lowers your taxable income, so you owe less federal income tax. If you contribute through payroll, the money also escapes the 7.65% FICA tax. And once inside, the balance grows tax-free and comes out tax-free for qualified medical expenses. The calculator adds up the first two effects for the current year and projects the third over time.

2026 contribution limits

To contribute, you must be covered by a qualifying high-deductible health plan (HDHP) and have no disqualifying coverage. For 2026 the IRS limits are $4,400 for self-only coverage and $8,750 for family coverage. If you're 55 or older, you can add a $1,000 catch-up contribution. This tool caps your entry at the limit that matches your coverage and age and tells you when you've hit it.

Why payroll contributions save more

The single biggest difference in HSA savings is how you contribute. Money routed through your employer's payroll under a cafeteria plan is excluded from wages for both income tax and FICA — Social Security (6.2% up to the wage base) and Medicare (1.45%). A direct contribution you deduct on your return still saves income tax, but you've already paid FICA on those wages. For a worker in the 22% federal bracket, the payroll route can mean an effective discount near 30% on every dollar, versus about 22% for a direct contribution.

The tax-free growth most people miss

Many people treat an HSA as a spending account, paying this year's medical bills straight out of it. But if you can cover medical costs from other cash and leave the HSA invested, it becomes a stealth retirement account: contributions go in pre-tax, grow tax-free for decades, and come out tax-free for the medical expenses nearly everyone faces later in life. The "tax-free value over time" figure shows what contributing the same amount each year could grow to at your chosen return — none of it taxed along the way.

Fitting the HSA into your plan

Because HSA dollars are so efficient, many advisers suggest funding it right after capturing any 401(k) employer match. Frequently asked questions

What is the HSA triple tax advantage?
An HSA is taxed favorably three times: contributions are pre-tax (lowering your taxable income, and avoiding FICA when made through payroll), the balance grows tax-free, and withdrawals for qualified medical expenses are tax-free. No other account combines all three.
What are the 2026 HSA contribution limits?
For 2026, the IRS limit is $4,400 for self-only coverage and $8,750 for family coverage. If you are age 55 or older, you can add a $1,000 catch-up contribution. You must be covered by a qualifying high-deductible health plan (HDHP) to contribute.
Do HSA contributions avoid Social Security and Medicare tax?
Only when contributed through payroll deduction under your employer's cafeteria plan. Those contributions skip the 7.65% employee FICA tax in addition to income tax. Contributions you make directly and deduct on your tax return save income tax but not FICA.
Does my state tax HSA contributions?
Most states follow the federal rule and exclude HSA contributions from state income tax. A few — notably California and New Jersey — do not, so contributions are still subject to state tax there. Enter your state's marginal rate (or 0% if your state has no income tax or fully conforms) to estimate the state savings.
Can I invest my HSA?
Yes. Many HSA providers let you invest the balance above a small cash threshold in mutual funds or ETFs. Because growth and qualified withdrawals are tax-free, a long-invested HSA can become a powerful retirement-health account. This calculator projects that tax-free growth over the years you choose.
